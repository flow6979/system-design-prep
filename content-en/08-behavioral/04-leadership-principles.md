# Amazon LPs & Company Values

**In one line:** every big company scores the behavioral round against its own values. Amazon does it most explicitly (Leadership Principles), but Google, Meta, Microsoft and Indian product companies do the same, just with different names.

> **Example:** An Amazon SDE loop has 4–5 rounds, and every round has 1–2 LP questions along with DSA/design. Each interviewer is assigned 2–3 LPs. Perfect coding with weak LP stories means no offer. It is not a "soft round".

## ⭐ Amazon's 16 Leadership Principles

⭐ = asked most often in SDE interviews.

| LP | In one line |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Customer Obsession** | Start with the customer and work backwards. Earn and keep customer trust |
| ⭐ **Ownership** | Think long term, never say "that's not my job". Act on behalf of the whole company |
| **Invent and Simplify** | Bring new ideas and make things simpler |
| **Are Right, A Lot** | Good judgment, and try to disprove your own beliefs |
| ⭐ **Learn and Be Curious** | Keep learning, explore new areas |
| **Hire and Develop the Best** | Spot talent, mentor and grow people |
| **Insist on the Highest Standards** | Keep the bar high, don't let defects pass downstream |
| **Think Big** | Big vision, bold direction |
| ⭐ **Bias for Action** | Speed matters. Make reversible decisions quickly, take calculated risks |
| **Frugality** | Do more with less. Constraints breed innovation |
| ⭐ **Earn Trust** | Listen carefully, speak candidly, admit your mistakes, show respect |
| ⭐ **Dive Deep** | Go into the details, be sceptical when anecdotes and data differ. No task is beneath you |
| ⭐ **Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit** | Disagree respectfully, then commit fully once the decision is made |
| ⭐ **Deliver Results** | Focus on key inputs, deliver on time with quality. Even after setbacks |
| **Strive to be Earth's Best Employer** | Make the workplace safe, inclusive and a place to grow |
| **Success and Scale Bring Broad Responsibility** | Own the wider impact (society, environment) of your decisions |

## ⭐ Most-asked LPs: questions and what to show

### ⭐ Customer Obsession
- **Question:** "Tell me about a time you went above and beyond for a customer." / "A time you used customer feedback to change something."
- **Show:** the customer can be an end user or an internal team. How you measured their pain (tickets, metrics) and what you changed.

### ⭐ Ownership
- **Question:** "Tell me about a time you took on something outside your area." / "A time you made a sacrifice for the long term over the short term."
- **Show:** you saw a problem with no owner, picked it up, and took it to the end. A long-term fix, not just a patch.

### ⭐ Dive Deep
- **Question:** "Tell me about a time you solved a problem by going deep into data." / "A time the metrics said one thing but your gut said another."
- **Show:** logs, traces, query plans, numbers. How you reached the root cause, and 2–3 hypotheses you rejected.

### ⭐ Bias for Action
- **Question:** "Tell me about a time you made a decision without complete information."
- **Show:** how you weighed the risk, a reversible decision, a rollback plan. What waiting would have cost.

### ⭐ Deliver Results
- **Question:** "Tell me about a time you delivered under a tight deadline." / "A time you hit a major obstacle."
- **Show:** goal, obstacle, prioritisation, the final number. Even with a scope cut, the core result was delivered.

### ⭐ Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit
- **Question:** "Tell me about a time you disagreed with your manager or team." / "A time you committed to a decision you didn't agree with."
- **Show:** pushback with data, respect, and full commitment after the decision (no "I told you so").

### ⭐ Earn Trust
- **Question:** "Tell me about a time you had to deliver bad news." / "A time you admitted a mistake."
- **Show:** transparency, telling people early, admitting the mistake, repairing the relationship.

### ⭐ Learn and Be Curious
- **Question:** "Tell me about a time you learned a new technology quickly." / "What's something you learned recently on your own?"
- **Show:** structured learning, applied it, shared it with the team.

## Amazon loop format

- **Rounds:** 4–5, each ~60 min: ~15–20 min LP + ~40 min coding/design.
- **Bar raiser:** a trained interviewer from another team who protects the hiring bar. Probes LPs the hardest, 15 min on one story.
- **Don't repeat a story** across rounds. Interviewers share notes.
- **Format:** STAR, focus on "I", data. See [STAR Method](../08-behavioral/01-star-method.md).

## Google: Googleyness and GCA

| Signal | Meaning | How it is tested |
|---|---|---|
| **GCA** (General Cognitive Ability) | Structuring and reasoning through a new problem | Open-ended / hypothetical: "How would you do X?" |
| **Googleyness** | Comfortable with ambiguity, humble, collaborative, user focus, doing the right thing | Past behaviour + hypothetical: "A teammate is missing deadlines, what do you do?" |
| **Leadership** | Influence without a title | "A time you led without authority" |
| **Role-related knowledge** | Tech depth | Coding / design rounds |

- Google also asks **hypothetical** questions. Answer them, then say "this happened to me..." and back it with a real story.
- A hiring committee reads written feedback, so clear, structured answers matter.

## Meta: behavioral round

- There is a dedicated behavioral round (for SDEs too). Focus areas: **conflict resolution, working in ambiguity, growth (taking feedback), driving results, collaboration**.
- Values: Move Fast, Focus on Long-Term Impact, Build Awesome Things, Be Direct and Respect Your Colleagues.
- The level (E3/E4/E5) is also decided in this round: how big your scope was (own task → team → multiple teams).
- Tip: make the scope clear in every story: "This affected 3 teams".

## Microsoft: growth mindset

- Culture: **growth mindset** ("learn-it-all, not know-it-all"), customer obsession, collaboration (One Microsoft), diversity & inclusion.
- Questions: "A time you failed and what you learned", "How did you handle feedback?", "A time you helped a teammate grow."
- The last round ("as appropriate", with a senior leader) is often behavioral + motivation.
- Tip: in failure stories, spend more time on "what I learned and what I changed".

## Indian product companies

| Company | What they focus on | Typical questions |
|---|---|---|
| **Flipkart** | Ownership, delivery at scale, project deep dive in the HM round | "Explain your best project's architecture and your role", "Any incident during a sale?" |
| **Swiggy / Zomato** | Speed, ownership, customer impact, ambiguity | "How did you ship a vague requirement?", "How did you move a metric?" |
| **Razorpay / PhonePe / CRED** | Correctness, ownership, transparency, high bar | "Tell me about a mistake that reached production", "A time you pushed back on a decision?" |
| **Startups (Series A–C)** | Generalist, bias for action, ownership beyond role | "What did you do outside your role?", "A decision with little information?" |

- At Indian companies the **HM round** is the most important behavioral round. Usually a project deep dive + 3–4 culture questions + "why leaving".
- In a founder/CTO round, the focus is more on motivation, long-term fit and ownership.

## ⭐ One story → multiple LPs

You don't need a separate story for every LP. One strong story can cover 3–4 LPs; just **change the emphasis**.

**Story:** "During the Diwali sale, checkout latency hit 6 sec. I found a full table scan in the coupon query, mitigated it in 4 min with a feature flag, and drove 3 permanent fixes in the postmortem."

| LP | Which part to stress | Opening line |
|---|---|---|
| Dive Deep | Ruling out via rollback, slow query log, query plan | "Let me tell you about an incident where my first hypothesis was wrong..." |
| Bias for Action | The quick cache-only flag decision, risk vs waiting | "We had to mitigate before we knew the root cause..." |
| Ownership | Postmortem, query review process for coupon creation | "After the fix, I made sure it couldn't happen again..." |
| Customer Obsession | 82% payment success, users' failed checkouts | "Every minute, thousands of customers couldn't pay..." |
| Earn Trust | Stakeholder update every 10 min, transparent RCA | "During the incident, the first thing I set up was communication..." |

- Use a story only once in the same round. Different stories for different rounds is better.
- That's why 6–8 stories are enough. For the mapping see [Story Bank](../08-behavioral/05-story-bank.md).

## Say this in the interview

> "Let me give you an example where the data proved my first assumption wrong..." (a strong opening for Dive Deep / Are Right A Lot)

> "I raised my concern with data, but once the decision was made I committed fully..." (the core of Disagree and Commit)

## Common mistakes

- Treating LPs as "theory" and skipping them. At Amazon they carry as much weight as coding.
- Naming the LP ("this shows my Ownership") without showing the behaviour. Show the behaviour; the interviewer will apply the label.
- The same story in every round.
- Answering Google's hypothetical questions with only theory, without backing them with a real example.
- A generic "why this company" answer without researching the company.
